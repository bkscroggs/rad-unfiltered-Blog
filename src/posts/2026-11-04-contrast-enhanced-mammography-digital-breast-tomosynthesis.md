Dense fibroglandular breast tissue remains one of breast imaging's central diagnostic challenges: it simultaneously increases baseline breast cancer risk and masks non-calcified masses on standard 2D full-field digital mammography (FFDM). While Digital Breast Tomosynthesis (DBT) has largely replaced 2D-only screening across major practices by reducing tissue-overlap recalls, dense breasts still leave a sensitivity gap that has historically pushed patients toward supplemental ultrasound or breast MRI.

Contrast-Enhanced Mammography (CEM)—pairing standard low-energy mammographic views with dual-energy recombined iodine images after intravenous iodinated contrast administration—has emerged as a practical, clinic-ready vascular imaging alternative directly on existing mammography gantries.

> "Contrast-enhanced mammography pairs the microcalcification sensitivity of high-resolution mammography with functional tumor neovascularity—inside a ten-minute mammography suite slot."
> <cite>Women's Imaging Clinical Practice Digest</cite>

## Comparing Supplemental & Diagnostic Modalities

Understanding where DBT, CEM, whole-breast ultrasound, and abbreviated breast MRI fit requires balancing diagnostic sensitivity against patient tolerance, contraindications, and scheduling access.

| Modality | Primary Mechanism | Approximate Exam Time | Key Clinical Advantage | Notable Limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| Digital Breast Tomosynthesis (DBT) | Multi-angle 3D X-ray reconstruction | 10 min | Superior architectural distortion & mass margin clarity | Lower sensitivity in extremely dense (BI-RADS D) tissue |
| Contrast-Enhanced Mammography (CEM) | Dual-energy iodine subtraction + low-energy FFDM | 10–15 min | High sensitivity comparable to MRI; sees microcalcifications simultaneously | Requires IV iodinated contrast; mild radiation increment |
| Supplemental Screening Ultrasound | High-frequency acoustic reflection | 20–30 min | Zero ionizing radiation; no IV contrast | Higher false-positive biopsy rate; operator dependent |
| Abbreviated Breast MRI (AB-MRI) | Gadolinium dynamic vascular enhancement | 10–15 min | Highest overall sensitivity; zero ionizing radiation | Cost, claustrophobia, scanner backlog, gadolinium constraints |

## Where CEM Is Reshaping Diagnostic Workflows

### Inconclusive Callbacks and Preoperative Extent of Disease

In diagnostic clinics, CEM excels as a rapid problem-solving tool when DBT and targeted ultrasound leave equivocal findings—or when a patient with newly diagnosed breast cancer cannot tolerate MRI due to claustrophobia, metallic implants, or scanner wait times. Because the low-energy acquisition is diagnostically equivalent to standard 2D mammography, radiologists evaluate suspicious pleomorphic calcifications and enhancing masses in a single unified study.

### Monitoring Neoadjuvant Systemic Therapy

Recent multi-institutional studies continue to assess CEM alongside breast MRI for evaluating residual tumor burden after neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Early tumor shrinkage and persistent non-mass enhancement correlate closely with MRI assessments while keeping imaging accessible in outpatient breast centers.

## Looking Ahead: Synthetic Views and Dose Optimization

Ongoing vendor refinements focus on integrating tomosynthesis planes directly with contrast-enhanced acquisitions and reducing glandular dose through improved synthetic 2D reconstructions—bringing functional breast imaging closer to routine outpatient availability without sacrificing interpretive confidence.
